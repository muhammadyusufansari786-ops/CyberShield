Password Best Practices

Follow these guidelines to create and manage strong passwords

📏

Length Matters

Use at least 12 characters. Longer passwords exponentially increase entropy and resist brute-force attacks.

🔤

Mix Character Types

Combine uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Each character type adds to the possible combinations.

🚫

Avoid Common Patterns

Don't use dictionary words, sequences (123456), repeated characters, or personal information.

🔄

Unique Per Account

Never reuse passwords. If one service is breached, your other accounts remain secure.

🔐

Use a Password Manager

Generate and store unique complex passwords for every account. You only need to remember one master password.

📱

Enable MFA Everywhere

Multi-factor authentication adds a critical second layer. Even if your password is compromised, attackers can't access your account.

Understanding Password Entropy

What the strength score actually means

What is Entropy?

Password entropy measures unpredictability in bits. Higher entropy means more possible combinations an attacker must try.

Entropy (bits) Strength Time to Crack (est.) Example
< 28 Very Weak Instant password123
28 - 35 Weak Minutes to hours P@ssw0rd
36 - 59 Fair Days to years Tr0ub4dor&3
60 - 127 Strong Centuries correct-horse-battery-staple
128+ Very Strong Millennia+ X7#mK9$pL2@vN4!qR6

💡 Pro Tip

Passphrases (4-5 random words) are both memorable and high-entropy. Example: correct-horse-battery-staple has ~44 bits of entropy.
